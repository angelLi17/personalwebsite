As someone who has a hard time remembering past experiences, I've decided this place is going to be where I document my thoughts. And it will be public on the internet, but hopefully not many people will find this.

. . .
I absolutely enjoyed my first semester (summer) at Berkeley so, so much. My family never travels anyway, and I got to taste freedom and independence. I consider myself a hyper-independent individual, and being at home feels suffocating. Probably because of my parents, whose judgment and reactions and even words and expressions I subconsciously am vigilant to. I met so many amazing people, got to create the life that I want for myself—living in an apartment, grocery shopping, going to the gym, studying in the libraries.

First semester has me absolutely exhausted. Started with roommate problems (clashing sleep schedules) to club applications (hello mlab final 32 candidates and then rejected), it jumped to midterm season and I've just been catching up since. It's on campus from 8 (if I actually wake up) to 8 pm and half the time I'm falling asleep in lecture. I had the urge to go home and went home I think 3 times already even though I regret it every time. I feel like my safe space and agency is gone at Berkeley, and I will try my best to change that again. I need to be able to trust myself to make the choices that I know I want but are hard. I mean it's totally logical that if I did it over summer, I can do it again, but I just don't feel like I'm the same person as I was idk a month ago even. But hey it's a new motivation to get a job and learn to drive. Maybe I'll move to New York or Boston after college, but I need to feel like *I* own myself-body soul and spirit-not my parents. 

On Relearning
I think a lot of it is because I'm worried about maximizing my freshmen year, and I regret that I couldn't help but start neglecting things like physical health or mental health again. I thought I learned this lesson in junior year of high school, I mean it was in my college essays, but I guess lessons can be re-learned. Coincidentally, all three techs I'm taking this year are kind of revisiting and relearning topics I've taken in high school: physics e&m, linear algebra, dsa. I'm picking up stuff I missed the first time round, like the line density theory which is the origin of Gauss' law. I hate rewatching and rereading shows and books because I already know what happens, but in practice learning things a second is almost always valuable.

On Communication
In no world could I ever be a debater, lawyer, or businessman. I struggle to advocate for myself or have confidence in my own grievances. I start talking to others about my problems and I don't know if it's the way I describe the situation or the fact that I'm so easily swayed by what others say I lose sight of my own grievance, hurt, and struggle and completely by in to others. Like I trust anyone except myself. I also cannot confront people, and I've found texting to be a wonderful alternative, where I can pick my words carefully and edit paragraphs 8 times before I send.

. . .
Recently I've been discovering how much of an introvert I am, as in that I get energy from being alone. I'm thankful that I somehow find that alone time in the evenings even though I live with 3 other people. I don't find myself seeking new social circles, and I think that's totally okay. In fact, I feel a bit burdened but grateful for the ones that I've already joined—my friend group from the summer, my church community, Cal Hacks, course staff. I've noticed it's much easier with a group of people instead of many single connections I have that require so much effort to reach out to every individual — high school friends, summer camp friends, etc. College and school is really the thing that binds me to a lot of people I know, and without the structure of having classes together or studying or eating together I'm not sure if we'd stay together, but I definitely will put in the effort to. But really, having people drift in and out is normal! 

A lot of the groups that I have joined have been saying the same thing to me. "We want to get to know you better!" But what does that even mean? Isn't it just spending more time together? I don't really know what it means to be "known" to others, since I guess that requires knowing myself first. But it's also hard because I don't like small talk--it feels quite ingenuine and I'm rather sick of putting up fronts.
